Russia and Ukraine agreed to the largest prisoner exchange of their war as two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended on Wednesday with no breakthrough on ending the fighting, Reuters reported. US envoy Jared Kushner said the numbers would be finalised on Thursday, when he and Steve Witkoff travel to Moscow to meet President Vladimir Putin.

At the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told more than 100 leaders that "the climate crisis is outrunning us," calling it "the path to mutually assured destruction," Reuters reported. He proposed a climate justice compact, partly funded by taxes on oil companies, while the United States stayed away for a second year.

The Associated Press called Mississippi's US Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom, who narrowly defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 5,000 votes, NPR reported. Colom is the first Democrat to win a Mississippi Senate seat since 1982, and his victory lifts Democrats to 55 seats, with Alaska still undecided.

US stocks rebounded on Wednesday, with the Nasdaq up 1.6% to 25,466 and the S&P 500 up 1.3%, Reuters reported, as CoreWeave shares jumped about 25% on strong earnings. Brent crude rose 1.4% to $92.76 a barrel amid uncertainty over US-Iran diplomacy.

Iran's Foreign Ministry designated Argentina's navy and air force as "terrorist organizations," Reuters reported, a reciprocal move after Buenos Aires labelled Iran's Revolutionary Guard a terrorist entity in April. Argentina's Foreign Ministry said it "categorically rejects" the decision.

CNN reported on Wednesday that British counterterrorism police had arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer in England on suspicion of assisting Russian intelligence. The officer served at NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps headquarters near Gloucester; no charges have been announced.
